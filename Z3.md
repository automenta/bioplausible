Z3 references **Konrad Zuse’s Z3**, completed in 1941, which is widely recognized as the world’s first fully functional, programmable, Turing-complete digital computer. 

The naming of your "Z3 Benchmark" is not just a historical nod; it is a precise mathematical analogy for what you are trying to achieve with the Joint Architecture. Here is exactly why the Z3 is the perfect historical patron saint for your "Fixed Weights, Changing Algorithm" benchmark:

### 1. The "No Conditional Branching" Constraint
The most famous quirk of Zuse’s Z3 was its hardware limitation: **it did not have conditional branching** (no `if/else` statements that could change the instruction pointer). The punched celluloid tape could only execute sequentially. 

To get the Z3 to perform conditional logic, programmers had to use an arithmetic workaround: they had to compute *both* possible paths and then multiply them by the boolean condition to mask out the unwanted path (i.e., `Result = (Condition * Path_A) + ((1 - Condition) * Path_B)`). 

**The Computronium Parallel:** 
In standard deep learning, routing (like Mixture of Experts) usually relies on hard, discrete `argmax` routing, which breaks the gradient and requires complex workarounds (like the Gumbel-Softmax trick or REINFORCE). Your Z3 benchmark forces the network to do exactly what Zuse did: compute a differentiable, soft mixture of operators mediated by the fast state $\psi$:
$$T_t = \sum_k g_k(\psi_t) T_k$$
You are forcing the network to achieve task switching via continuous arithmetic masking, without altering the underlying physical substrate ($\theta$).

### 2. Hardware vs. Tape (The $\theta$ vs. $\psi$ Divide)
In the Z3, the physical hardware (the wiring of the ~2,600 electromechanical relays and the arithmetic unit) was **strictly fixed and immutable**. You couldn't "rewrite" the machine's weights. The "algorithm" lived entirely in the sequence of operations on the punched tape. 

**The Computronium Parallel:**
*   **The Z3 Relays** = Your persistent weights **$\theta$** (frozen, representing the slow, energetically expensive physical substrate).
*   **The Z3 Punched Tape** = Your fast plasticity state **$\psi$** (dynamic, representing the fast, intra-episode memory that routes the computation).

### 3. Turing Completeness via Meta-Dynamics
For decades, computer scientists debated whether the Z3 was actually Turing-complete because of its lack of conditional branches. It wasn't until 1998 that Raúl Rojas mathematically proved that the Z3 *was* Turing-complete, simply by writing a clever, looping program on the tape that simulated a Turing machine's state transitions. The "algorithm" was emergent from the tape, not the hardware.

**The Computronium Parallel:**
Your Z3 Benchmark asks the exact same fundamental question of neural networks: *Can a network with strictly frozen parameters ($\theta$) achieve universal, multi-task algorithmic behavior purely through the dynamics of its fast plastic state ($\psi$)?*

This metaphor perfectly encapsulates the core philosophy of **Computronium**. However, **evolving internal activation states ($\psi$) is fast, cheap, and local** (like reading a tape or letting an energy landscape settle).
